You don't need a photo studio for a passport photo. A modern smartphone camera is more than capable. Here's how to get it right on the first try.

## What you need

- Smartphone with rear camera (8+ MP)
- White wall or white sheet
- Natural light (window)
- Someone to take the photo (selfies don't work for passports)

## Setup

1. **Find a white wall.** If you don't have one, hang a white sheet.
2. **Position near a window.** Stand 3-4 feet from the window for even, diffused light.
3. **Remove glasses.** US passports don't allow glasses. Other countries may, but it's safer without.
4. **Plain clothing.** Avoid white shirts (blends with background) and busy patterns.

## Taking the photo

1. **Camera at eye level.** Have the photographer hold the phone at your eye height.
2. **Distance: 4-6 feet.** Far enough to include head and shoulders with room to crop.
3. **Portrait mode off.** You want a flat, undistorted image.
4. **No zoom.** Use the main lens, not the telephoto or ultrawide.
5. **Burst mode.** Take 10-15 shots and pick the best one.

## What to check

- **Sharpness.** Zoom in on the eyes — they should be tack-sharp.
- **Shadows.** No harsh shadows on the face or behind the head.
- **Expression.** Neutral or natural smile, eyes open.
- **Head position.** Looking straight at the camera, not tilted.

## Processing

1. Upload the best photo to a passport photo tool.
2. Select your country's spec.
3. The tool crops, positions your head, and resizes to the correct dimensions.
4. Download and print.

## Printing tips

- Print on matte or glossy photo paper
- Use a 4×6 print with multiple passport photos arranged on it
- Cut with scissors or a paper cutter for clean edges
