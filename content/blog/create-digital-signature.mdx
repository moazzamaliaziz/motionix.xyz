You need to sign a PDF contract, but you don't have a printer or scanner. A digital signature image solves this in seconds. Here's how to create one.

## Three ways to create a digital signature

### 1. Draw with your mouse or trackpad
Open a signature maker tool and draw your signature with your cursor. The tool records stroke pressure and speed for a natural look.

### 2. Draw with your finger on a touchscreen
On a phone or tablet, draw your signature with your finger. This produces the most natural-looking result.

### 3. Type your name
Type your name and choose from multiple handwritten-style fonts. Quick and consistent, but less personal.

## Getting a transparent PNG

A transparent PNG is essential for dropping signatures into documents:
- No white box around the signature
- Works on any document background color
- Looks like a real ink signature

## Adding signatures to PDFs

1. **In Adobe Acrobat:** Tools → Fill & Sign → Place Signature
2. **In Preview (Mac):** Tools → Annotate → Signature
3. **In Google Drive:** Open PDF → Add signature (if available)
4. **In Word:** Insert → Pictures → Select your signature PNG

## Legal considerations

Digital signature images are **not** the same as cryptographic digital signatures:
- **Image signatures** are visual only — they don't verify identity
- **Cryptographic signatures** (DocuSign, Adobe Sign) provide audit trails
- For legal contracts, use a proper e-signature platform
- For internal documents and forms, image signatures are fine

## Best practices

- **Use black ink.** Most documents have white backgrounds.
- **Keep it consistent.** Use the same signature across all documents.
- **Save at high resolution.** 300 DPI minimum for print quality.
- **Store securely.** Your signature is personally identifiable information.
