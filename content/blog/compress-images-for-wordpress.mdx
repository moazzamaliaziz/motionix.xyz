Most CMS platforms have upload size limits and compress images on the server anyway. Pre-compressing gives you control over the quality and ensures faster page loads.

## CMS upload limits

| Platform | Default limit | Recommended image size |
|----------|---------------|----------------------|
| WordPress | 2-64 MB (host-dependent) | < 500 KB |
| Squarespace | 20 MB | < 500 KB |
| Wix | 15 MB | < 500 KB |
| Shopify | 20 MB | < 300 KB |
| Webflow | 4 MB | < 300 KB |

## Why pre-compress

1. **Control over quality.** CMS compression algorithms vary. Pre-compressing ensures consistent quality.
2. **Faster uploads.** Smaller files upload faster, especially on slow connections.
3. **Better page speed.** Serve optimized images immediately instead of waiting for server-side processing.
4. **Storage savings.** CMS storage is often limited or metered.

## WordPress-specific tips

- **Maximum upload size:** Check Settings → Media for your host's limit.
- **Thumbnail generation:** WordPress creates 3 sizes by default (thumbnail, medium, large). Pre-compressing the original saves storage for all copies.
- **WebP support:** WordPress 5.8+ supports WebP natively. Use it instead of JPEG.
- **Lazy loading:** WordPress lazy loads images by default. Don't disable it.

## Compression workflow

1. **Resize first.** WordPress displays images at max 1200px wide (theme-dependent). Don't upload 4000px images.
2. **Compress to 80-85 quality.** Visually identical, 60-70% smaller.
3. **Use WebP if supported.** 25-35% smaller than JPEG.
4. **Upload and verify.** Check the image looks good at display size.

## Common mistakes

- **Uploading raw camera photos.** A 6000×4000 JPEG at quality 95 is 8-12 MB. Resize and compress first.
- **Using BMP or TIFF.** Convert to JPEG or WebP before uploading.
- **Not compressing at all.** Every 100 KB saved improves page load by ~0.1 seconds on 3G.
