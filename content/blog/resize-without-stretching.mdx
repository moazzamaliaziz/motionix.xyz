Resizing an image to the wrong aspect ratio stretches or squishes it. Here's how to resize correctly while maintaining proportions.

## What is aspect ratio?

Aspect ratio is the relationship between width and height:
- **1:1** — Square (Instagram, profile photos)
- **4:3** — Standard photo (most phone cameras)
- **16:9** — Widescreen (YouTube, TV)
- **3:2** — DSLR photo (most cameras)
- **9:16** — Vertical video (TikTok, Stories)

## Why images get distorted

Distortion happens when you change one dimension without proportionally changing the other. A 4000×3000 image resized to 500×500 becomes squished horizontally.

## How to resize correctly

1. **Lock the aspect ratio.** Most tools have a "lock" or "constrain proportions" option.
2. **Set one dimension.** The tool automatically calculates the other.
3. **Crop if needed.** If the target aspect ratio differs from the original, crop instead of stretching.

## Common aspect ratio mismatches

| Original | Target | Problem | Solution |
|----------|--------|---------|----------|
| 4:3 photo | 1:1 square | Stretched or cropped | Crop to center |
| 16:9 video | 4:3 portrait | Letterboxed or cropped | Crop to subject |
| 3:2 photo | 16:9 widescreen | Stretched horizontally | Crop top/bottom |

## Cropping vs stretching

**Always crop rather than stretch.** A cropped image looks intentional. A stretched image looks like a mistake.

When cropping:
1. Identify the subject (person, product, etc.)
2. Keep the subject centered or in the rule-of-thirds position
3. Crop symmetrically when possible
