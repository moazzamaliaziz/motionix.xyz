You have a great group photo, but you need just one person from it. Maybe it's the only recent photo of someone, or you need a headshot from a team event. Here's how to extract individuals cleanly.

## The challenge of group photos

Group photos create unique problems for background removal:

- **Overlapping subjects.** One person's shoulder is behind another person's head.
- **Varying depths.** People in the front are sharp, people in the back may be soft.
- **Complex backgrounds.** Events, offices, and outdoor settings have busy backgrounds.
- **Cropped compositions.** You may need to crop tightly before processing.

## Step-by-step extraction

1. **Crop around the person.** Include some background around their outline, but exclude other people where possible.
2. **Upload the crop.** The AI processes the cropped image, not the full group photo.
3. **Check the mask.** Pay attention to shoulders and arms that overlap with others.
4. **Download and composite.** Place the extracted person on a new background.

## When it works well

- Person is clearly separated from others
- Good lighting on the individual
- Simple or blurred background
- No overlapping limbs or accessories

## When it doesn't work well

- Person is partially behind another person
- Complex, busy background similar to their clothing
- Very tight crop with no breathing room
- Motion blur from camera or subject movement

## Alternatives to extraction

If the group photo doesn't yield a clean cutout:

- **Ask for a solo photo.** A phone selfie is better than a bad extraction.
- **Use the group photo as-is.** Crop to a headshot without removing the background.
- **Composite manually in Photoshop.** For one-off needs, manual editing may be faster.
