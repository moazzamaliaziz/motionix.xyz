Pet photos are the hardest subjects for background removal. Fur creates thousands of tiny edges, ears have complex outlines, and pets never hold still. Here's how to get clean results anyway.

## Why pets are tricky

- **Fur is semi-transparent at the edges.** Each strand creates a tiny edge that the model has to decide on.
- **Ears and tails have complex outlines.** Curved, pointed shapes with varying thickness.
- **Pets move.** Motion blur creates soft edges that confuse segmentation models.
- **Similar colors.** A white cat on a light background is a nightmare for any tool.

## Photography tips for pet background removal

1. **Use natural light.** Harsh flash creates red-eye and harsh shadows.
2. **Plain background.** A solid-colored blanket or wall behind the pet.
3. **Contrast helps.** Dark pet on light background, or vice versa.
4. **Get on their level.** Shoot from the pet's eye height for better framing.
5. **Burst mode.** Take 10-20 shots and pick the sharpest one.

## Processing pet photos

1. Upload the sharpest photo to a browser-based tool.
2. Check the mask preview — look at ears, tail, and whiskers.
3. If the edges are fuzzy, try a different photo from the burst.
4. Download as transparent PNG.

## Common issues and fixes

| Issue | Fix |
|-------|-----|
| Ears cut off | Crop tighter before processing — less background = less confusion |
| Fur looks jagged | Use a higher-resolution input photo |
| Whiskers disappear | These are the hardest — accept some loss or use Photoshop for refinement |
| Shadow included | Shoot with even lighting to minimize shadows |
