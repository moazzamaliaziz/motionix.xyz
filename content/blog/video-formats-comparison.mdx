Video formats are more complex than image formats. Here's a practical guide to choosing the right one.

## Video format comparison

| Format | Codec | Quality | File size | Browser support | Use case |
|--------|-------|---------|-----------|----------------|----------|
| MP4 | H.264 | Good | Medium | 99% | Web, email, social |
| MOV | H.264/H.265 | Excellent | Medium-Large | 80% | Apple ecosystem, editing |
| WebM | VP9/AV1 | Excellent | Small | 85% | Web, streaming |
| AVI | Various | Varies | Large | 50% | Legacy, editing |
| MKV | Various | Varies | Medium | 30% | Archival, media servers |

## When to use MP4

- **Web video.** Universal browser support.
- **Email attachments.** Accepted by all email clients.
- **Social media.** All platforms accept MP4.
- **General purpose.** When in doubt, use MP4.

## When to use MOV

- **Apple workflows.** Native format for iPhone video and Final Cut Pro.
- **Professional editing.** Preserves quality through multiple edit cycles.
- **When quality matters most.** MOV supports higher bitrates than MP4.

## When to use WebM

- **Web delivery.** 20-30% smaller than MP4 at equivalent quality.
- **When browser support is acceptable.** 85% coverage in 2026.
- **Streaming.** Optimized for progressive download.

## Codec matters more than format

The codec (H.264, H.265, VP9, AV1) determines quality and file size:
- **H.264:** Universal compatibility, good quality, medium file size.
- **H.265:** 40% smaller than H.264, limited browser support.
- **VP9:** 20-30% smaller than H.264, good browser support.
- **AV1:** 50% smaller than H.264, growing support.

## Conversion tips

- **MOV → MP4:** Usually just a container change, no quality loss.
- **AVI → MP4:** May require re-encoding, some quality loss.
- **WebM → MP4:** For compatibility with older browsers.
