If you've ever had a passport application returned because your photo was the wrong size, you're not alone. The State Department processed over 19 million US passport applications in 2025 — and a meaningful fraction get bounced for photo issues.

Here's where the major country rules stand in 2026.

## United States

- **Size:** 2 × 2 inches (600 × 600 px at 300 DPI)
- **Head:** 50–69% of frame height
- **Background:** Pure white
- **Expression:** Neutral or natural smile
- **Glasses:** Not allowed (changed in 2016)
- **Digital edits:** No AI editing, no background synthesis (2025 rule)

The US is the strictest about digital manipulation. Strict mode tools that only crop and position are fine. AI background swaps are not.

## United Kingdom

- **Size:** 600 × 750 px (35 × 45 mm printed)
- **Head:** 29–34 mm from chin to crown
- **Background:** Plain light-coloured (white or light gray)
- **Expression:** Neutral, mouth closed
- **Glasses:** Allowed if no glare

The UK accepts a slightly wider background range than the US.

## India

- **Size:** 350 × 350 px (2 × 2 inches)
- **File size:** 20–50 KB
- **Background:** White
- **Format:** JPEG only
- **Head:** Centered, 70-80% of frame

India has the tightest file size constraints. You may need to compress after resizing.

## Schengen (EU)

- **Size:** 413 × 531 px (35 × 45 mm)
- **Head:** 32–36 mm from chin to crown
- **Background:** White or light grey
- **Recency:** Taken within last 6 months
- **Format:** JPEG, high quality

## Common rejection reasons

1. **Wrong size.** Each country has specific pixel and print dimensions.
2. **Bad background.** Off-white, gray, or patterned backgrounds get rejected.
3. **Glasses glare.** Even transparent lenses can reflect light.
4. **Too old.** Photos must be recent — embassies compare to your current appearance.
5. **Digital manipulation.** AI-edited photos are flagged by some countries' software.

## How to take a passport photo at home

1. Stand 3 feet from a white wall.
2. Use natural light from a window (no harsh shadows).
3. Camera at eye level, 4-6 feet away.
4. Neutral expression, eyes open, mouth closed.
5. No glasses, no head coverings (unless religious).
