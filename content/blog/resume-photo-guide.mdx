The resume photo debate is nuanced. In some countries, it's expected. In others, it's discouraged. Here's what the data says and how to make the right choice for your situation.

## Should you include a photo?

**Yes, if:**
- You're applying in Europe, Asia, or the Middle East (photos are standard)
- You're in a client-facing role (sales, consulting, hospitality)
- You're in a creative field (design, marketing, media)
- The job posting specifically asks for one

**No, if:**
- You're applying in the US or UK (photos can trigger bias concerns)
- You're in a technical role where appearance is irrelevant
- The company uses blind hiring practices

## What makes a good resume photo

1. **Professional attire.** Dress one level above the company's dress code.
2. **Neutral background.** White, light gray, or blurred office setting.
3. **Head and shoulders.** No full-body shots, no selfies.
4. **Recent photo.** Taken within the last 2 years.
5. **Natural expression.** Approachable, not overly serious.

## Photo specs for resumes

| Use case | Size | Aspect ratio |
|----------|------|-------------|
| Resume header | 150×200 px | 3:4 |
| LinkedIn profile | 400×400 px | 1:1 |
| CV (European) | 200×250 px | 4:5 |
| Business card | 300×300 px | 1:1 |

## Common mistakes

- **Casual photos.** Beach photos, party photos, or cropped group photos.
- **Over-filtered.** Heavy Instagram filters look unprofessional.
- **Too small.** Blurry photos from being stretched.
- **Wrong crop.** Full-body shots that show too much background.

## How to take a resume photo at home

1. Stand against a white or light gray wall.
2. Face a window for even lighting.
3. Wear professional attire.
4. Have someone take the photo from the chest up.
5. Upload to a resume photo tool for cropping and background cleanup.
