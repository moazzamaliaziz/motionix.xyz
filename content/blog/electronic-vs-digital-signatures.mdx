The terms "electronic signature" and "digital signature" sound interchangeable, but they're fundamentally different technologies with different legal implications.

## Electronic signatures

An electronic signature is any electronic indication of intent to agree. It can be:
- A typed name in an email
- A scanned image of a handwritten signature
- A click on an "I agree" button
- A drawn signature on a touchscreen

**Legal status:** Recognized in most countries under laws like ESIGN Act (US), eIDAS (EU), and similar legislation.

**Security:** Minimal. Easy to forge, no built-in verification.

## Digital signatures

A digital signature uses cryptographic technology to:
- Verify the signer's identity
- Ensure the document hasn't been tampered with
- Create an audit trail with timestamps
- Provide non-repudiation (signer can't deny signing)

**Legal status:** Stronger legal standing. Considered equivalent to a wet signature in most jurisdictions.

**Security:** High. Uses public-key cryptography (PKI) with certificate authorities.

## Comparison

| Feature | Electronic | Digital |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Identity verification | None | Cryptographic |
| Tamper detection | None | Built-in |
| Audit trail | Optional | Automatic |
| Legal weight | Moderate | Strong |
| Cost | Free or low | Higher |
| Ease of use | Very easy | Moderate |

## When to use electronic signatures

- Internal documents and approvals
- Informal agreements
- Low-risk transactions
- When convenience matters more than security

## When to use digital signatures

- Legal contracts
- Financial documents
- Government filings
- Healthcare records
- When identity verification is critical
